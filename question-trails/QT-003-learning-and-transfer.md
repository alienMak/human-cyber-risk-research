# QT-003 — Learning, repetition and transfer

**Recorded in this repository:** 18 October 2026  
**Earlier documented context:** 9 September 2026 — Related longitudinal learning and transfer questions were documented in research discussions by 9 September 2026; the first occurrence remains unverified.  
**Origin:** Reconstructed from existing public research questions about repeated scenarios and longitudinal behavior. This is a proposed research chain, not a verbatim conversation.  
**Status:** Open.

## Brief

Improvement on a repeated security exercise might indicate learning. It might also indicate memory of the specific exercise, familiarity with the interface or a difference in scenario difficulty. To claim transfer, we need observations beyond the original exercise.

## Question chain

| Step | Question | Working answer | Follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 | Does a better result on the same scenario prove learning? | It proves a change in performance on that task, not why the change occurred. | Could the participant simply remember the scenario? |
| Q2 | Would an unfamiliar scenario demonstrate transfer? | It provides stronger evidence if the scenario is genuinely novel and comparably difficult. | How do we establish comparability? |
| Q3 | What does a delayed follow-up add? | It tests persistence, but outside exposure and attrition may confound interpretation. | What controls and records are needed? |
| Q4 | Could feedback improve one task but not others? | Yes, as a hypothesis. Task-specific improvement and broader transfer are distinct outcomes. | How should those outcomes be reported? |
| Q5 | What would challenge a familiarity-only explanation? | Repeated gains on matched novel tasks after delay, relative to a suitable comparison group. | Would those gains survive changes of channel and context? |

## Competing explanations

1. Participants acquired a more generalizable skill.
2. Participants memorized cues, stories or interface patterns.
3. Differences reflect scenario difficulty, selection, prior experience or other exposure.

## Position and next test

Treat improvement on repeated material as task-specific until appropriately matched novel tasks and delayed follow-up support transfer. The competing explanations remain open. Any proposed test should predefine outcomes, controls and limitations.
