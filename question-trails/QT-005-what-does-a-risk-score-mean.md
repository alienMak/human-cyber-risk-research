# QT-005 — What can a behavioral score legitimately tell us?

## Brief

A compact score can summarize observations, but it can also conceal missing opportunities, different task difficulties and measurement error. Before asking whether the number is useful, ask what the observations actually represent.

## Question chain

| Step | Question | Working answer | Next question or implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 | Does a single click reveal stable susceptibility? | No; it is one outcome in one context. | What repeated observations would help? |
| Q2 | Do faster responses necessarily indicate confidence or recklessness? | No. Familiarity, accessibility, interface delay and task complexity can all affect time. | What context is needed? |
| Q3 | Can different scenarios be compared as though equally difficult? | Not without accounting for scenario difficulty and audience relevance. | How do we avoid unfair comparisons? |
| Q4 | Does a combined indicator become valid merely because it is numerically precise? | No. Precision of calculation is not evidence of construct validity or predictive utility. | What would validation require? |
| Q5 | What would falsify the score's practical value? | Poor reliability, weak association with aligned outcomes, unstable performance across populations or harmful false confidence would all challenge it. | How should uncertainty be reported? |

## Competing explanations

1. Observed differences reflect stable decision tendencies.
2. Observed differences reflect scenario/context variation.
3. Observed differences reflect measurement noise, missingness or interface effects.

## Provisional position and confidence

A score is a hypothesis about what observations summarize, not proof of stable individual risk. High confidence in the need for validation; no proprietary metric or formula disclosed.
