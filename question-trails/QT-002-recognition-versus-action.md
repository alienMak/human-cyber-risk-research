# QT-002 — Recognizing a threat is not the same as acting safely

**Earlier documented context:** 6 August 2026 — Related recognition-versus-action research framing was discussed on 6 August 2026; this is not a verified first occurrence of the question.  
**Origin:** Reconstructed from the existing public research agenda on recognition, confidence and behavior under pressure; not a verbatim conversation.  
**Status:** Open research trail; reconstructed conceptual analysis, not a verbatim transcript.

## Brief

A person can recognize a suspicious request and still comply. Conversely, someone may reject a legitimate request without having recognized any threat. A click/no-click result alone does not tell us which process occurred.

## Reasoning in more detail

Imagine two participants who both refuse to click. One noticed a suspicious sender and checked through an independent route. The other ignores every unexpected message, including legitimate ones. The same final action conceals very different abilities. That is why a lower click rate, on its own, cannot establish better threat recognition.

The reverse problem matters too. Someone might correctly identify an inconsistency but comply because they believe delaying the request will cause trouble at work. That is a possible explanation, not something a non-click or click record proves. To tell these cases apart, the research would need evidence about the person's interpretation and the options actually available, while recognizing that self-reported explanations can be reconstructed after the event. The existing recognition-to-action agenda discusses related studies, but the different populations and outcomes should not be treated as one pooled result.

**Hypothetical counterexample (not observed participant data):** An employee correctly suspects a message but clicks because they fear delaying an urgent request from a supervisor. Another employee does not suspect anything yet ignores the message because they are busy. The clicker may have better recognition, while the non-clicker has the safer recorded outcome. Neither click status alone identifies the mechanism.

## Competing explanations

1. Knowledge deficit: users misidentify the request.
2. Action barrier: users suspect danger but lack a usable verification or refusal path.
3. Measurement artifact: a non-click is misclassified as an informed safe decision.

## Provisional position and confidence

Keep initial interpretation, perceived social costs, practical access to verification, observed checking and final action conceptually separate. Moderate confidence in construct separation; low confidence in specific causal mechanisms or cross-setting predictive validity.
