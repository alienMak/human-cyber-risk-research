## Research timeline
- 1950s–1970s: origins of computing and artificial intelligence
- 1980s–1990s: personal computing, networks and early online trust
- 2000s: internet fraud, phishing and verification
- 2010s: human factors, behavioral interventions and usable security
- 2020s: generative AI, synthetic media and human–AI decisions

The dates above describe historical subjects, not podcast publication dates. Episodes must be individually verified before a claim is recorded as evidence.

## Questions worth tracing across episodes
1. Which persuasion mechanisms remain similar across telephone, email and AI-generated communications?
2. When do accessible verification mechanisms change observed decisions rather than stated intentions?
3. How does AI-generated confidence affect willingness to check a claim independently?

## Two complementary research lenses

1. **Trust decisions:** How people recognize authority, judge credibility, and choose whether to verify a claim.
2. **Manufactured credibility:** How interface design, institutions, communication channels, impersonation and AI-generated material make a claim appear trustworthy, legitimately or deceptively.

For each episode, distinguish a speaker's account from independently established evidence. A podcast alone cannot validate an intervention or a causal mechanism.
