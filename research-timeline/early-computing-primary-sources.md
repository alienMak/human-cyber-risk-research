# Early computing and trust: primary-source anchors

**Checked 13 October 2026.** These are dated historical sources, not measurements of how people trusted technology.

## 1955–1956: artificial intelligence as a research program

The [1955 Dartmouth proposal](https://www-formal.stanford.edu/jmc/history/dartmouth.pdf), by John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Nathaniel Rochester and Claude Shannon, set out a summer investigation of artificial intelligence. [Dartmouth's history](https://ai.dartmouth.edu/founding-and-future-ai) documents the 1956 meeting. The proposal concerns possible machine intelligence; it does not establish public reliance on AI.

## 1982: internet email conventions

[RFC 821](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc821/) specifies the Simple Mail Transfer Protocol. [RFC 822](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc822/) specifies message format. These primary technical standards help explain why transport and message presentation must be distinguished from independently verifying an asserted sender.

## 1989–1990: linking information on the web

Tim Berners-Lee's [information management proposal](https://cds.cern.ch/record/369245/files/dd-89-001.pdf), archived by CERN, describes a distributed hypertext approach. The [CERN archive record](https://cds.cern.ch/record/1405411) identifies the original March 1989 proposal. Hypertext helps readers navigate information; a link is not itself proof of a claim's accuracy.

## Unresolved historical questions

What changed in the verification process as communication moved from institutionally managed systems to the open web? Which identity mechanisms emerged in the 1990s and 2000s? These milestones alone cannot establish a historical trend in human trust.
