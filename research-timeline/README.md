# Technology, trust and verification: a historical timeline

**Checked 13 October 2026.** This is a selective, source-linked chronology, not a comprehensive history. It distinguishes the **date of the development** from the date of the source used to discuss it. Historical entries outside the 2016–2026 literature-review window belong here, not in that review's screening counts.

| Period | Documented milestone | What changed in the trust environment | Evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1969 | ARPANET went online in late October, alongside other early networks | Networked communication created new opportunities for exchanging information across machines and institutions | [Computer History Museum's 1969 timeline](https://www.computerhistory.org/timeline/1969/), historical museum account. It does not establish how individual users evaluated online trust. |
| 2019–2020 | NIST researchers developed and published a Phish Scale | An apparent sender or plausible work-related premise can be evaluated in context; raw click rates need difficulty context | [NIST's 2019 workshop record](https://www.nist.gov/publications/phish-scale-rating-human-phishing-message-detection-difficulty); [2020 journal article](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/journal/2020/09/categorizing-human-phishing-detection-difficulty-a/final). The journal's initial validation was limited to its available datasets. |
| 2022 | Controlled investigation of visible email-header cues | Displaying more ostensibly diagnostic information does not guarantee users interpret it correctly | [Zheng and Becker, SOUPS 2022](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2022/presentation/zheng), publisher abstract checked; not a universal claim about all email interfaces. |
| 2023 | Usability evaluation of email checking and nudging features | The usability of independent verification is a design question, not just an education problem | [Zheng and Becker, SOUPS 2023](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2023/presentation/zheng). Formative study of 27 professional email users; not a field effectiveness trial. |
| 2023 | NIST published AI RMF 1.0 | Trustworthiness became an explicit risk-governance concern for AI development and deployment | [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework), a voluntary framework, not proof of an AI system's trustworthiness. |
| 2024 | NIST released its generative-AI profile | Generative-AI-specific risks received dedicated risk-management guidance | [NIST AI RMF resource page](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework), guidance rather than empirical evidence that mitigation works. |

## What this timeline does not yet establish

A date is not a causal explanation. The evidence here supports particular developments, not a general claim that people became more or less trusting over time. The 1950s–1960s history of AI, the 1980s–2000s growth of online identity and the arrival of generative conversational tools require separately verified primary or archival sources before adding dated claims.

## Questions arising

- When does a technology's familiar appearance become mistaken for proof of identity or reliability?
- Which changes increase the ability to independently verify a claim, and which merely add more credibility cues?
- Do new AI interfaces reproduce older authority problems or create materially different ones?
