# Human–AI trust: credibility is not correctness

**Scope:** The relationship between human decisions and apparently authoritative AI-generated outputs. This is a research agenda supported by public guidance and adjacent human-security studies, **not** a claim that an AI trust experiment has been completed.

## Four distinctions to keep separate

1. **System capability:** Whether an AI system produces accurate outputs for a defined task.
2. **Perceived credibility:** Whether a person believes the output or interface.
3. **Reliance behavior:** Whether the person acts on it, delegates to it or independently verifies it.
4. **Institutional accountability:** Who evaluates, deploys, monitors and corrects the system.

[NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework), published in 2023, is voluntary guidance for managing risks and trustworthiness in AI systems. NIST's 2024 generative-AI profile extends risk-management guidance to generative AI. Neither establishes that fluent language or a safety label makes an individual output correct.

## Connecting older research to newer interfaces

[Zheng & Becker (2022)](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2022/presentation/zheng) show, in the studied email conditions, that simply displaying potentially useful authenticity information did not improve detection. [Zheng & Becker (2023)](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2023/presentation/zheng) evaluated the usability of tools that make checking easier. These are **analogies**, not experimental evidence about conversational AI.

The key research tension: a system may make information easier to obtain while making it harder for a user to recognize when independent verification is necessary.

## Testable questions, not results

- When source citations are present, do users verify the cited original or merely report higher confidence?
- Does an explicit verification route improve observed checking behavior compared with a warning alone?
- Do people respond differently to a mistaken recommendation when it appears to come from a person, a rule-based tool or a conversational AI system?
- How do role, task familiarity and time pressure moderate these responses?

## Guardrails for future study

Predefine observable outcomes; distinguish intention from action; vary only justified factors; avoid deception without ethical review; do not expose real participants or confidential systems; and report failure cases, uncertainty and limits of generalization.
