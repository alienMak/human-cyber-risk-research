# Research gaps worth testing

These are candidate research gaps suggested by the first screened studies, not claims that nobody in the wider literature has investigated them.

| Question | Why current evidence is not enough | What a stronger VISHWAR study might test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does learning transfer between attack vectors? | Many evaluations measure email recognition or clicking | Compare new and familiar scenarios across phishing, messaging and other vectors using predefined outcome measures |
| How does risk change across repeated sessions? | Short-term improvement and durable transfer are often conflated | Run longitudinal follow-ups with controlled scenario difficulty and repeat exposure |
| Does faster responding indicate risk? | Response speed has several possible explanations | Analyze speed alongside confidence, accuracy and task complexity |
| Are reports a better outcome than mere non-clicks? | Reporting opportunities and organizational incentives differ | Define when reporting is possible and measure reporting separately |
| What does a combined risk score mean? | Weighting observable measures does not itself validate a latent construct | Pre-register composite-score assumptions and test reliability, calibration and predictive validity |
| Does role-playing generalize outside a training session? | Intentions, confidence and field behavior are distinct | Compare experimental interventions with matched real-world or ecologically valid transfer tasks |
| How does attacker-manufactured familiarity alter a decision? | Contextual cues may vary by channel, role and culture | Randomize sender cues, urgency, authority and verification friction |
| Are simulations fair and valid across groups? | Demographic, accessibility and situational confounds affect outcomes | Audit scenario difficulty, accessibility and measurement invariance before individual comparisons |

## Design cautions

VISHWAR's exploratory 20-person pilot is useful for generating questions, not for asserting causal superiority. There should be no individual labeling from an unvalidated score. Any public study must protect participant information and disclosure-sensitive IP. Confidence intervals, sample characteristics, missingness and uncertainty should be reported wherever measurements warrant them.
