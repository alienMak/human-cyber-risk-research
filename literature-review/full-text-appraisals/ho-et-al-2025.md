# Ho et al. (2025): conventional training in a large field experiment

**Source:** [IEEE S&P publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1109/SP61157.2025.00076); [open author-hosted paper](https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~grantho/papers/oakland2025_phishing-training.pdf). *Understanding the Efficacy of Phishing Training in Practice*, IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy 2025, pp. 37–54. **Access:** Publisher abstract and author-hosted paper opening verified 13 October 2026; this is a **partial appraisal**, not a complete full-text methods/statistics extraction.

## Reported study

The paper reports an eight-month randomized controlled experiment across ten simulated phishing campaigns involving more than 19,500 employees in a large healthcare organization. It assesses annual awareness training and embedded anti-phishing training. The publisher abstract reports no significant relationship between recent annual-training completion and simulated-phishing failure; embedded training produced very small absolute differences in failure rates, and most users spent little time on embedded material.

## Interpretation

This is strong evidence against assuming that **these conventional training implementations** necessarily improve the measured simulated-phishing outcome. It is not proof that all education is useless, nor that a different behavioral intervention will work.

## What remains to appraise

- Exact randomized unit and comparison groups for each component.
- Absolute rates, uncertainty intervals, subgroup analyses and statistical assumptions.
- Distinguish observational comparisons involving annual-training recency from randomized embedded-training contrasts.
- Scenario difficulty, repeat exposure, missingness and possible contamination.
- Generalization beyond one healthcare institution and ten simulated email campaigns.

## Relevance

Compare with [Reinheimer et al. (2020)](reinheimer-2020.md) only after harmonizing **email classification**, **simulated phishing failure**, follow-up timing and training format. Apparently conflicting findings may measure different things.
