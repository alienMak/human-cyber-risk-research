# Reinheimer et al. (2020): how long does phishing education last?

**Source:** [USENIX SOUPS 2020 publisher record](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2020/presentation/reinheimer); [open-access PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/soups2020-reinheimer_0.pdf).  
**Evidence access:** Original publisher abstract and the paper's published measurement timeline inspected 13 October 2026. This is a **partial appraisal**, not a completed section-by-section full-text appraisal.

## Study and outcomes

The authors report a field investigation in a German public-administration organization involving 409 employees. They assessed phishing and legitimate-email identification after an education program and after four different reminder formats. The publisher summary reports improvement immediately and at four months, but not at six months; video and interactive reminders performed relatively well in the setting.

The paper's measurement timeline includes baseline, immediate post-tutorial, four-month, six-month, eight-month and twelve-month observations, with reminder interventions at the six-month point. That timing matters: an immediate knowledge improvement is not evidence of indefinite retention.

## Interpretation and limitations

The measured outcome concerns classifying email examples, not observed loss in real phishing incidents. The setting is one public-administration organization. The reported results cannot be assumed to generalize to other workforces, communication channels or generative-AI interactions. Attrition, allocation, exact test items, effect sizes and uncertainty estimates need closer extraction before stronger causal or transfer claims.

## Follow-up extraction still required

Record sample sizes at each measurement point, intervention allocation, exact message-selection procedures, statistical tests, reported effect sizes and confidence intervals, attrition and ethical disclosures. Compare these outcomes with studies measuring simulated clicks or reporting **only after** harmonizing their definitions.

**Research implication:** If a defensive intervention improves email classification for a few months, what actually transfers to unfamiliar decisions later? That remains a question, not a demonstrated property of any simulation platform.
