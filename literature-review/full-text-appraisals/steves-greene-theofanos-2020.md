# Steves, Greene and Theofanos (2020): difficulty is part of the outcome

**Original article:** [Categorizing Human Phishing Detection Difficulty: A Phish Scale](https://csrc.nist.gov/csrc/media/projects/Usable-Cybersecurity/images-media/Categorizing%20human%20phishing%20difficulty.pdf), *Journal of Cybersecurity* 6(1), tyaa009, DOI [10.1093/cybsec/tyaa009](https://doi.org/10.1093/cybsec/tyaa009). **Access:** Original 16-page article inspected 13 October 2026; targeted full-text appraisal, not independent replication.

## What the authors tested

The authors proposed a way to categorize phishing-message detection difficulty using **observable message cues** and **premise alignment with a target audience**. They applied it to **ten workplace phishing exercises**, seven previously reported and three previously unpublished (paper pp. 2–3, Methods). Eight exercises had roughly 70 or more participants; two had roughly 5,000 each. This is an exploratory evaluation across exercises, not a randomized trial establishing causal effects of training.

The paper explicitly describes its scale as preliminary and calls for broader validation with different phishing messages. The authors emphasize that work role and local context change which cues a person is likely to notice.

## What can be inferred

Comparing raw click rates across differently difficult scenarios can mislead. A low click rate can reflect an easy or familiar scenario, not necessarily strong protection. The scale attempts to make exercise difficulty visible to those interpreting training results.

## Methodological cautions

- The difficulty classification includes judgments about audience premise alignment; reproducibility depends on knowing that audience.
- Observed click rate is an exercise-level outcome; it does not establish an individual-level stable vulnerability trait.
- The paper's ten exercises do not validate the method for telephone, in-person interactions or AI conversations.
- A priori difficulty categorization and post-hoc click rate are different quantities; avoid circular claims that one perfectly predicts the other.
- No independent extraction or recalculation of all tables was performed in this note.

## Research implication

Any comparison of human security decisions should document the task's difficulty and contextual relevance before interpreting differences as changes in participants. This is a **general methodological implication**, not an endorsement of any private scoring method.
