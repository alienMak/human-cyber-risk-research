# What exactly are we measuring?

The literature repeatedly reveals a problem with comparisons across awareness studies: different outcomes get called “effectiveness.”

| Outcome | What it measures | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quiz or knowledge score | Recall or recognition under a test format | Safe behavior in a real attack |
| Click or interaction rate | Specific interaction with a lure | Whether the participant reported, recognized or later corrected the attempt |
| Credential submission | Disclosure behavior in a particular exercise | General susceptibility without controlling for scenario context |
| Reporting rate | Use of an available reporting mechanism | Complete detection accuracy where reporting access or expectations differ |
| Response time | Speed for the presented decision | Confidence, deliberation or quality without additional measures |
| Intention or self-efficacy | Stated willingness or confidence | Observed future action |
| Scenario choice | Decision inside a simulation | Generalization to real incidents without validation |
| Repeated choice patterns | Within-person behavior across tasks or sessions | Stable individual risk independent of scenario difficulty |

## Comparison design for future VISHWAR studies

1. Pre-register the main behavioral outcome and what counts as an unsafe decision.
2. Measure scenario difficulty separately from participant responses; consider the [NIST Phish Scale](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/journal/2020/09/categorizing-human-phishing-detection-difficulty-a/final) for email lures, while acknowledging it is not automatically validated for physical or conversational scenarios.
3. Compare groups or time points using matched scenarios, avoiding answer memorization and uncontrolled changes in lure difficulty.
4. Record exposure, feedback, role, language, accessibility needs and reporting opportunities.
5. Report uncertainty and missingness, not only averages or composite scores.
6. Distinguish improvement on familiar tasks from transfer to a different attack vector.
7. Validate any risk composite against an independently defined outcome before treating it as a predictive instrument.

A 20-person exploratory pilot can inform feasibility and future hypotheses. It is not enough, by itself, to establish generalizable intervention effectiveness or validate an individual risk score.
