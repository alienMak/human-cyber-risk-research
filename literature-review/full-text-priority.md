## Recognition, context and perceived consequences (15 October 2026)

**High priority — full text not yet appraised:** [Weidhaas, von Preuschen & Distler (SOUPS 2026)](https://doi.org/10.60882/cispa.32775066). Publisher abstract: qualitative simulated-workplace study, N=41, observation/interviews/questionnaires. Not clicking was sometimes perceived as a reputational risk. Extract scenario, recruitment, coding, participant quotes, counterexamples and limitations before inferring mechanisms. **ABSTRACT-ONLY.**

**High priority — full text not yet appraised:** [Distler (CHI 2023)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3581170). In-situ deception study, N=14, examining workplace context and reporting decisions. Verify observation conditions and interpretation of behavior. **ABSTRACT-ONLY.**

**High priority — full text not yet appraised:** [Li et al. (SOUPS 2026)](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2026/presentation/li). Survey of 1,074 university students across five US universities; publisher abstract reports differences in responses to scam scenarios despite no observed group difference in suspicious-cue detection. Extract exact operational definitions, effect sizes and confound controls. **ABSTRACT-ONLY.**

These papers suggest a question, not an established causal pathway: when recognition and final action diverge, which contextual and social consequences matter? Keep recognition, reporting, verification and compliance as separate outcomes. No private implementation or participant data are included.
