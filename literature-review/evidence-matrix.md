# Evidence matrix: human cyber risk (2016–October 2026)

This is a growing **scoping evidence matrix**, not a claim that every paper published in ten years has been screened. The first batch was checked against publisher, conference, NIST, or preprint records on 10 October 2026. Most entries were assessed at abstract level; do not treat this as a full-text critical appraisal.

| Year | Study | Source | Method and coverage | Reported finding or focus | VISHWAR research connection | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2017 | Nicholson, Coventry & Briggs — [Can we fight social engineering attacks by social means? Assessing social salience as a means to improve phish detection](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2017/technical-sessions/presentation/nicholson) | SOUPS | Experiment; signal detection | Sender salience improved detection; confidence was poorly calibrated for phishing | Contextual cues and confidence calibration | Reviewed abstract |
| 2017 | Lastdrager et al. — [How Effective is Anti-Phishing Training for Children?](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2017/technical-sessions) | SOUPS | School intervention, follow-up | Reported immediate improvement but phishing detection gains eroded after four weeks | School module, retention | Reviewed program summary |
| 2020 | Steves, Greene & Theofanos — [Categorizing Human Phishing Detection Difficulty: A Phish Scale](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/journal/2020/09/categorizing-human-phishing-detection-difficulty-a/final) | Journal of Cybersecurity / NIST | Method development, enterprise data | Lure difficulty and work context affect interpretation of click rates; broader validation needed | Scenario difficulty normalization | Reviewed abstract |
| 2020 | Reinheimer et al. — [An investigation of phishing awareness and education over time: When and how to best remind users](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2020/presentation/reinheimer) | SOUPS | Field study, 409 employees | Detection improved directly and at four months but not at six; video/interactive reminders performed well | Longitudinal follow-up and reminder timing | Reviewed abstract |
| 2021 | Dawkins, Jacobs & Greene — [The NIST Phish Scale: Method for rating human phishing detection difficulty](https://www.nist.gov/publications/nist-phish-scale-method-rating-human-phishing-detection-difficulty) | NIST / M3AAWG | Method guidance from NIST program data | Offers a practical approach to interpreting varying phishing detection difficulty | Pre-classification of scenario challenges | Reviewed abstract |
| 2024 | Chen et al. — [What Motivates and Discourages Employees in Phishing Interventions: An Exploration of Expectancy-Value Theory](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2024/presentation/chen) | SOUPS | Seven focus groups, 34 employees | Value, feedback, privacy, and social environment shape engagement and reporting | Reporting behavior and organizational setting | Reviewed abstract |
| 2024 | Tabassum, Faklaris & Lipford — [What Drives SMiShing Susceptibility? A U.S. Interview Study of How and Why Mobile Phone Users Judge Text Messages to be Real or Fake](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2024/presentation/tabassum-sarah) | SOUPS | Interviews, 29 participants | People focused on content, format and links, often overlooking sender identifiers | SMS/vishing cross-channel decision cues | Reviewed abstract |
| 2024 | Chen et al. — [The Effects of Group Discussion and Role-playing Anti-phishing Training: Evidence from a Mixed-design Experiment](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2024/presentation/chen-poster) | SOUPS poster | Preregistered mixed experiment, 105 employees | Role-playing improved support-seeking intention relative to control; reporting and vigilance outcomes highlighted | Interactive training and observable reporting | Reviewed poster abstract |
| 2025 | Rozema & Davis — [Anti-Phishing Training (Still) Does Not Work: A Large-Scale Reproduction of Phishing Training Inefficacy Grounded in the NIST Phish Scale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19899) | arXiv preprint | Large operational study, 12,511 employees | No significant main effects on clicks or reports; phishing difficulty predicted variation | Distinguish training exposure from behavior changes | Reviewed abstract; preprint |
| 2025 | Authors as listed by publisher — [Understanding the Efficacy of Phishing Training in Practice](https://doi.org/10.1109/SP61157.2025.00076) | IEEE S&P | Eight-month randomized experiment, >19,500 employees | Conventional training had limited practical value on simulated-phishing outcomes | Control conditions and behavioral endpoints | Reviewed publisher abstract |
| 2025 | Greco et al. — [Improving Phishing Resilience with AI-Generated Training: Evidence on Prompting, Personalization, and Duration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01893) | arXiv preprint | Two controlled studies, N=480 | Pre-post learning gains; complex personalization did not outperform generic material | Separate learning accuracy from field behavior | Reviewed abstract; preprint |
| 2026 | Spicer & Nurse — [What makes people susceptible to phishing attacks: A systematic literature review and future research agenda](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cose.2026.105096) | Computers & Security | Systematic literature review; 54 included of 4,402 screened | Integrates individual, environmental and organizational factors; intention-behavior gap | Build a multi-factor research model | Reviewed abstract |
| 2026 | Madeira & Alves — [Designing effective phishing awareness training: The role of feedback and engagement strategies](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1071581926001369) | International Journal of Human-Computer Studies | Quantitative and qualitative organizational evaluation | Immediate feedback reduced susceptibility; mandated participation presented motivational trade-offs | Feedback design and engagement | Reviewed abstract |
| 2026 | Malloy, Fang & Gonzalez — [Improving online anti-phishing training using cognitive large language models](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747563225002079) | Computers in Human Behavior | Learning intervention with cognitive LLM | Reported better learning outcomes using cognitive model-based selection and feedback | Adaptive scenarios with learning-aware feedback | Reviewed abstract |

## What isn't covered yet

This initial set does not provide continuous annual coverage of 2016, 2018, 2019, 2022 or 2023. Existing separate commentary notes partly cover 2019, but annual screening is incomplete. Papers about physical social engineering, voice/deepfake attacks, children, cross-vector simulations, cybersecurity culture, and fairness in behavioral risk scoring also require dedicated searching. Do not assign inclusion/exclusion counts until an auditable screening process is performed.
