# Appraising the evidence instead of just collecting it

A long bibliography is not the same thing as a reliable evidence base. For this review, I want each study assessed on what it measured, how the sample was recruited, what comparison was available, and what the authors can actually conclude.

## Appraisal rubric

Use these eight dimensions for a full-text paper review. Mark each **Yes**, **Partial**, **No**, or **Not reported**, accompanied by a page or section reference. Never calculate a pseudo-precise quality score from unanswered questions.

| Domain | Question to answer |
| --- | --- |
| Research design | Can the design answer the stated research question? |
| Sampling | Is recruitment described, and what population can the sample represent? |
| Outcome definition | Are clicks, reporting, knowledge, intentions and simulated decisions distinguished? |
| Confounding | Are lure difficulty, prior training, job role, language and exposure considered? |
| Measurement | Are instruments described and their reliability or validity assessed? |
| Analysis | Are uncertainty, missing data and multiple comparisons addressed? |
| Generalization | How closely does the task resemble the decision environment of interest? |
| Ethics and reproducibility | Are privacy safeguards and materials/data access explained where appropriate? |

## Evidence levels

- **Record checked:** Publication identity, date and source link checked.
- **Abstract screened:** Methods and claims extracted from the abstract; limitations may be incomplete.
- **Full text appraised:** Original paper read and documented using this rubric.
- **Replicated or triangulated:** Independent analysis or multiple comparable studies support an interpretation.

Only use a level when the work has actually been completed. Never silently turn an abstract screening into a full-text critical review.
