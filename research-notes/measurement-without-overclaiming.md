# Measuring a decision without overclaiming

I am interested in indicators such as risky choices, reporting behavior, response timing, repeated risk events, and consistency across different scenarios. Each provides part of the picture. None is the whole picture.

## The temptation of a single score

A combined score is convenient. It makes comparison easier and gives a dashboard something clear to display. But combining measures also means deciding which behaviors count more than others, how missing data should be handled, and how much uncertainty the result can hide.

A formula can be implemented long before it has been validated. That distinction matters.

## What I want to examine

**Response time.** Someone who acts quickly may recognize the situation immediately, or may not have thought about it carefully. The surrounding task matters.

**Repeat decisions.** Repeated risky choices may reveal a pattern, but repeated exposure can also change the participant's familiarity with the scenario.

**Consistency.** Someone might verify one type of request and accept another that uses different cues. That difference is interesting, although it shouldn't automatically be described as a stable personal trait.

**Reporting as a possible future research measure.** Reporting may be a meaningful response in scenarios where someone can actually report an event. The current VISHWAR choice flow does not provide a separate report button, so a grey or context-dependent choice should not be treated as reporting. I would need an explicit reporting opportunity and a clear operational definition before studying reporting behavior as a distinct outcome.

## Where I stand for now

I'd rather report several interpretable signals with their limitations than present an apparently precise measure that has not earned that precision. This is an area for research and validation, not an assumption I want to bury inside the software.
