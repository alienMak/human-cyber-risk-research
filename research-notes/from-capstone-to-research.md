# From a capstone to a research question

VISHWAR did not start as a GitHub project. I began planning the research in February 2025, and the development phase started during my cybersecurity capstone in August 2025.

The starting problem was fairly simple to describe but much harder to measure: someone may know the right answer in cybersecurity training and still make a different decision when the situation feels urgent, familiar, or credible.

That gap between knowledge and behavior is what I wanted to investigate.

## What changed when I started building

The capstone gave me a way to move beyond static examples. Instead of only presenting an attack and explaining what went wrong, I could place a decision inside a scenario, record the response, and explore what the decision might reveal.

That also introduced new questions. Does a correct answer show understanding? Does a quick response mean confidence or simply guessing? If a participant performs better after training, how much of that improvement carries into a different type of attack?

I don't think any of those questions can be answered by pointing to one dashboard number.

## What came after the capstone

The early study and subsequent development led to more work on behavioral indicators, repeated decisions, consistency across different forms of social engineering, and educational use in school settings.

Some of this work has involved platform development. Other parts have involved research framing, experiment design, analysis, writing, and producing simulation media. Not all of that work generates a code commit, but it is still part of the research history.
