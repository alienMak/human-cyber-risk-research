# How I approach research

I started this repository because I want to understand what happens when people make cybersecurity decisions under pressure. Building VISHWAR raised questions that I couldn't answer just by making the platform work. I needed to look more closely at the evidence, at what existing studies actually measured, and at the assumptions behind the metrics we use.

This page sets out how I want to approach that work. It isn't a claim that every question here has already been answered.

## Start with the question

I want each experiment or reading note to begin with a question worth investigating. A result is useful to me only if I can explain what it tells us and, just as importantly, what it doesn't.

I'm particularly interested in what aggregate metrics hide: why two people with similar scores might make very different decisions, what happens across repeated encounters, and how the circumstances surrounding a decision shape the response.

## Understand the evidence before drawing conclusions

When reading a paper, I try to look beyond its headline conclusion. Who participated? What did the researchers ask them to do? What was measured, and compared against what? Could another explanation fit the results?

I want to distinguish between papers I've examined in full and those for which I've only seen an abstract. A useful summary should not suggest more certainty than the available evidence allows.

I also want to understand where studies disagree. A contradiction may point to different populations, experimental settings, definitions or measurement methods rather than one study simply being wrong.

## Treat experiments as questions, not proof

The early experiments in this repository use constructed data. They're a way to examine the behavior of a measure or demonstrate an information gap, not evidence about how real people behave.

If I find an interesting pattern in synthetic examples, my next question is what it would take to test that pattern properly: a clearer definition, a better comparison, more realistic scenarios, repeated observations or a study involving consenting participants.

I don't want to mistake a calculation that works for a measure that has been validated.

## Write clearly, including the limitations

I prefer to explain an idea in straightforward language, with an example when it helps. I don't think a finding becomes more valuable by making it sound bigger than it is.

In my notes, I want it to be clear which points come from a paper, which are my interpretations, and which are questions I still have. Where something is missing or uncertain, I'd rather say so.

## Be deliberate about what I publish

I want this repository to be useful to other researchers, but not everything involved in building or studying VISHWAR belongs in a public repository. Unpublished technical mechanisms, confidential information, and research that may require intellectual-property review will stay outside the public work.

I also want to preserve an accurate research history. Not every reading session needs a commit, and not every experiment will produce an interesting result. Sometimes ruling out an assumption or finding a limitation is the progress.

## The question I keep coming back to

**Are we measuring what people actually do when a cybersecurity decision matters, or only what our available metrics make easy to count?**

That's the question I want this research to keep challenging.
