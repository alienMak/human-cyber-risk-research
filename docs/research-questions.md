# Research questions I'm working through

This is a starting list. I expect some of these questions to change as the work develops.

## Decisions under pressure

An attacker does not always need to convince someone through a message alone. They can shape what the person sees before that message arrives: the channel, the claimed authority, the timing, and the sense that something is urgent.

**Question:** Which parts of that surrounding context influence a security decision most strongly, and how could an experiment separate those effects?

## Learning versus recognizing a scenario

If someone makes a safer choice the second time they see a scenario, that is encouraging. But it doesn't automatically mean the learning will carry over to a new situation.

**Question:** What changes between sessions would help us distinguish transferable learning from familiarity with a specific story?

## Measuring behavior responsibly

A score can make patterns easier to discuss, but it can also hide uncertainty. Response time, choices, repeated mistakes, and consistency may each tell different stories.

**Question:** How do we communicate these signals without implying more precision than the evidence supports?

## Where this goes next

For each question, I want to document the hypothesis, what evidence would challenge it, the experiment design, and what the results actually showed. If I don't have results yet, I'll say so.
