# Literature watch

This is where I collect papers and other sources that make me rethink how people judge credibility, respond to social-engineering attempts and make decisions under pressure.

What interests me is not just what a paper concludes, but **how its authors arrived there**. I want to understand what was measured, who participated, how the study was designed and whether another explanation could account for the result.

Some notes come from full papers; others begin with an abstract or a shorter report. I distinguish those because they do not support the same depth of interpretation. Practitioner reports, interviews and podcasts can raise valuable questions, but they are not interchangeable with peer-reviewed evidence.

Each note should leave me with something concrete: a finding worth examining, a limitation that changes how I interpret it, or a question that might lead to a better experiment. The [source index](sources.md) helps me avoid losing track of work I have already considered.

My broader approach is set out in [How I approach research](../docs/research-writing-and-evidence-standard.md). Notes are part of an ongoing research record; they should not be read as a claim that I have personally conducted every analysis or endorsed every interpretation.
