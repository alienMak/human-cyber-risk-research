# Literature watch

I keep coming back to the same question in my work on VISHWAR: why does someone who understands a security risk sometimes make a different decision when the situation feels credible or urgent?

This folder follows research and reporting that might help answer that question. Papers, articles, interviews, podcasts and incident analyses are all useful, but they need different levels of scrutiny.

An item belongs here because it changes a research question, suggests a testable hypothesis, reveals a limitation in something I assumed, or makes me look at a security decision differently. Finding a new link alone isn't enough.

## How I use these notes

- Identify the original source, author, publication date and URL.
- Separate what the source actually says from what I infer from it.
- Explain what might connect to human cyber risk, decision-making, social engineering or simulation design.
- Record an open question or an experiment worth considering.
- Distinguish a draft interpretation from an opinion I have personally reviewed and endorsed.

**Important:** Notes generated by the daily research assistant are labelled *Draft for review*. They are suggestions in a writing style informed by my existing work, not a claim that I read or personally agreed with the source. Reviewed notes can be marked *Reviewed by researcher* only after I have actually checked them.

## Where to look

Peer-reviewed papers and preprints, conference proceedings, reputable security reporting, primary incident reports, and podcasts with identifiable guests and dates. Prefer original material over secondhand summaries. A podcast isn't a paper, and a preprint isn't automatically peer-reviewed.

See [the note template](TEMPLATE.md) and [source log](sources.md).

## Publishing and contribution history

The daily search is meant to discover useful new material, not to guarantee a number of commits or a shade of green. A source is skipped when it's repetitive, weakly supported, unrelated, or already covered. No filler, invented reactions, or backdated commits.
