# Does a correct phishing decision transfer beyond the training task?

**Source:** Reinheimer et al., [An investigation of phishing awareness and education over time: When and how to best remind users](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2020/presentation/reinheimer), SOUPS 2020, pp. 259–284.  
**Source type:** Peer-reviewed empirical paper.  
**Review status for this note:** Publisher record and abstract checked; the complete paper has not been independently reappraised in this note.  
**Recorded:** 21 October 2026.

## Evidence and its boundary

Reinheimer and colleagues evaluated phishing education and reminders with 409 employees at a German public-sector organization. The publisher reports better identification of phishing and legitimate emails immediately after the educational programme and four months later, but not a statistically significant improvement at six months. It also reports encouraging results for video and interactive reminders.

This is evidence about performance on the study's email-identification task. It is not, by itself, evidence that participants would safely handle an unfamiliar request under deadline pressure, report a mistake, or recognize social engineering in a different channel. Nor does a non-significant six-month comparison prove that every participant forgot the training.

## A question that follows

What if training reduces acceptance of suspicious messages but also increases rejection of legitimate ones? A measure of improvement should distinguish both errors rather than count suspicion alone as success.

Three competing explanations deserve testing: learned discrimination, short-lived vigilance, and familiarity with a particular assessment format. These explanations are not established findings of the paper.

A future consented study could present both legitimate and suspicious requests, use unfamiliar examples at delayed follow-up, and record verification and reporting separately from the first decision. Workload and scenario difficulty would need to be considered before attributing differences to learning.

This is a public literature question, not a claim of a completed experiment or a description of any private system.
