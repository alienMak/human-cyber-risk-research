# Can ordinary notifications make a security warning easier to ignore?

**Source:** [The Fog of Warnings: How Non-essential Notifications Blur with Security Warnings](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2019/presentation/vance)  
**Authors:** Anthony Vance, David Eargle, Jeffrey L. Jenkins, C. Brock Kirwan and Bonnie Brinton Anderson  
**Published:** August 2019  
**Source type:** Research paper, SOUPS 2019  
**Added:** October 2026

## The finding

The researchers investigated whether habituation to frequent non-security notifications transfers to a new security warning when the two look similar. Their online experiment found reduced attention and lower adherence to a security warning after exposure to similar non-security notifications. The authors distinguish this generalization from simple fatigue.

## Why this matters for human cyber risk

It is tempting to interpret the dismissal of a warning as a knowledge failure. But the interface can train someone to dismiss interruptions before a security warning even appears. In that case, the surrounding environment influences the decision without the attacker having to persuade the person directly.

A simulation that changes only the phishing message but holds every interface cue constant may miss this part of the problem.

## Where the argument needs care

The experiment examined notification similarity and warning adherence, not all real-world social engineering scenarios. Attention measured in an online task may not fully capture how people react during real work.

## Question to pursue

If a simulated security decision follows a series of familiar non-security prompts, does warning adherence change compared with a version of the same decision presented without those prompts?
