# Research questions: from plausible stories to observable tests

**Prepared 13 October 2026.** These are public, conceptual questions for future work, not preregistered studies, unpublished methods or claims of completed experimentation.

| ID | Question | What would count as evidence? | Principal confound or boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RQ-01 | Does an independent verification route improve observed checking compared with passive authenticity cues? | Randomized comparison of checking behavior and correct decisions | Different effort required, interface familiarity and task difficulty |
| RQ-02 | How does a request's contextual fit affect recognition of impersonation? | Matched communications differing in premise alignment, with verified outcomes | Role, prior exposure and realism |
| RQ-03 | Does the apparent origin of advice (person, conventional software, conversational AI) affect reliance? | Observed reliance and source checking under controlled information quality | User expectations and interface affordances |
| RQ-04 | Does a reminder's apparent short-term benefit persist across time and unfamiliar situations? | Follow-up behavior using comparable but not memorized tasks | Attrition, repeated exposure and baseline differences |
| RQ-05 | Which indicators of credibility are mistaken for independent proof of identity? | Structured task observation plus explanations, separating interpretation from action | Digital literacy, language, accessibility |
| RQ-06 | When do organizational verification procedures reduce friction versus merely transfer responsibility to employees? | Workflow observations, measured checking opportunities and error rates | Workload, incentives, availability of a trusted channel |

## Why these questions are grounded

RQ-01 and RQ-05 arise from the distinction between [Zheng & Becker 2022](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2022/presentation/zheng) and [Zheng & Becker 2023](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2023/presentation/zheng). RQ-02 and RQ-06 draw on the contextual-difficulty problem described by [Steves et al. 2020](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/journal/2020/09/categorizing-human-phishing-detection-difficulty-a/final). RQ-04 is motivated by [Reinheimer et al. 2020](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2020/presentation/reinheimer). RQ-03 is an **open extension** to AI settings, not an inference that these email studies measured AI reliance.

## Standards for eventual investigation

Define the outcome before collecting data. Record scenario context and difficulty. Separate clicking, reporting, confidence, intention and independent verification. Address sampling, ethics, missingness and uncertainty. Do not publish participant records, proprietary platform internals or unreviewed potentially patent-sensitive techniques.

This list does not amend the existing [literature-review protocol](../literature-review/protocol.md) or claim that an experiment has been conducted.
